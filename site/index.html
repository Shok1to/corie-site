Core Investment Vehicles

Five investment products, each built for a specific role.

Retirement & Tax Deferral

RRSP

Registered Retirement Savings Plan

2026 Max: $33,810

Designed to build long-term retirement savings while lowering your current income tax bill. Contributions are tax-deductible today, and investments grow completely tax-deferred until withdrawn—ideally during retirement when your marginal tax rate is lower.

  • Contribution Room: 18% of prior-year earned income up to $33,810 for 2026, plus unused room carried forward indefinitely.
  • Upfront Tax Deduction: Deduct contributions against your highest marginal tax bracket during peak earning years.
  • HBP & LLP Access: Borrow tax-free for a first home under the Home Buyers’ Plan (HBP) (up to $60,000) or education under the Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP), subject to scheduled repayments.
  • Spousal RRSP Strategy: Contribute to a spousal RRSP to equalize retirement income and lower combined household taxes later.
Maturity Deadline: Dec 31 of year you turn 71 See RRSP retirement rules →
First-Home Accumulation

FHSA

First Home Savings Account

$8,000/yr · $40,000 Cap

The FHSA combines the best tax advantages of both an RRSP and a TFSA for eligible first-time homebuyers: contributions are tax-deductible like an RRSP, and qualifying withdrawals toward your first home—including all investment growth—are 100% tax-free like a TFSA.

  • Contribution & Carry-Forward: $8,000 annual limit up to a $40,000 lifetime cap. Unused room carries forward (up to $8,000/year) once the account is opened.
  • No Repayment Required: Qualifying home withdrawals never have to be repaid—and can be paired with the RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan.
  • 15-Year Window: Open for up to 15 years (or until December 31 of the year you turn 71, whichever comes first).
  • Tax-Free RRSP/RRIF Rollover: If you decide not to purchase a home, transfer your FHSA balance tax-free into your RRSP or RRIF without reducing existing RRSP contribution room.
Best Fit: First-time buyers within 15 years Tax-Deductible In · Tax-Free Out
Tax-Free Growth & Flexibility

TFSA

Tax-Free Savings Account

2026 Limit: $7,000

A versatile investment shelter capable of holding ETFs, stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and GICs. Funded with after-tax dollars, every dollar of interest, dividends, and capital gains compounds—and can be withdrawn at any time—completely free of tax.

  • Cumulative Room: $7,000 annual limit for 2026, plus unused room since 2009 (up to $109,000 cumulative for Canadian residents 18+ since 2009).
  • Replenishable Withdrawals: Withdraw funds tax-free anytime; the withdrawn amount is automatically added back to your contribution room on January 1 of the following year.
  • Zero OAS Clawback Impact: TFSA withdrawals do not count as taxable income and never reduce Old Age Security (OAS) or income-tested benefits.
  • Successor Holder Estate Advantage: Naming a spouse as successor holder preserves the tax-exempt status of the account upon death.
Earned Income Required? No (Age 18+ & SIN) 100% Tax-Free Liquidity
Education & Government Grants

RESP

Registered Education Savings Plan

$50k Cap + 20% CESG

A tax-deferred education savings plan designed to help parents, grandparents, and family members fund a child’s post-secondary education—supercharged by direct federal and provincial matching grants.

  • $50,000 Lifetime Contribution Limit: No annual contribution cap per beneficiary, allowing lump-sum or scheduled contributions up to $50,000 per child while investments compound tax-deferred.
  • Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG): Matches 20% on the first $2,500 contributed each year (up to $500/year and $7,200 lifetime maximum per child), with unused grant room carrying forward for catch-up contributions (up to $1,000 grant/year).
  • Additional Grants & Student-Taxed EAPs: Eligible families may also receive the Canada Learning Bond (CLB) (up to $2,000) and provincial incentives. At school, original principal comes out tax-free while grants and growth (EAPs) are taxed in the student’s hands—usually at zero or minimal tax.
  • RRSP Rollover Flexibility: Remains open up to 35 years; if a beneficiary does not pursue post-secondary education, up to $50,000 of accumulated investment income (AIP) can be rolled into the subscriber’s RRSP (subject to available RRSP room).
Plan Types: Individual & Family Plans Up to $7,200 Lifetime CESG Match
Unrestricted Wealth Building

Non-Registered Investments

Individual, Joint & Corporate Investment Accounts

Unlimited Capacity

Once your TFSA, RRSP, and FHSA limits are maximized—or when you require joint ownership, business liquidity, or unrestricted withdrawals—non-registered accounts offer unlimited investment capacity with preferential tax treatment on growth.

  • No Contribution Caps or Age Limits: Invest any amount at any stage of life with full liquidity and zero mandatory conversion deadlines.
  • Preferential Tax Rates: Only 50% of realized capital gains are included in taxable income (up to $250,000 annually for individuals), and eligible Canadian dividends receive the dividend tax credit.
  • Tax-Loss Harvesting: Capital losses can offset capital gains in the current year, be carried back 3 years, or carried forward indefinitely.
  • Strategic Asset Location: Ideal for holding tax-advantaged equity growth and Canadian dividend payers while sheltering interest income inside registered accounts.
Structures: Individual, Joint (JTWROS), Corporate Tax-Loss Harvesting Eligible

Retirement Transition Guide

What happens at retirement with RRSPs?

An RRSP is designed for accumulation, not permanent storage. Under CRA rules, an RRSP cannot remain open indefinitely—it must mature by December 31 of the calendar year in which you turn 71 (though you can begin converting earlier if you retire sooner).

Before that December 31 deadline, you must choose one—or a deliberate combination—of three maturity paths. Planning this transition ahead of time prevents an avoidable tax spike and protects government benefits like Old Age Security.

71
The Age 71 Rule
December 31 Deadline

If you do not elect a RRIF conversion or annuity purchase by December 31 of the year you turn 71, your financial institution must deregister the plan and pay out the entire balance as taxable income—potentially losing nearly half to top marginal tax brackets.

Planning Note: You can convert part or all of your RRSP before age 71 to smooth out lifetime taxes.

Three RRSP Maturity Options at Retirement

Select an option below to highlight its mechanics, tax impact, and ideal use case.

Option 01 · Most Flexible Most Popular

Convert to a RRIF

Registered Retirement Income Fund

Your RRSP investments transfer “in kind” into a RRIF without triggering tax, continue compounding tax-deferred, and shift into paying you structured retirement income.

  • • Mandatory Minimums: Begin the year after conversion (age 72 if converted at 71) based on CRA’s prescribed age schedule.
  • • No Maximum Withdrawal Cap: Withdraw extra funds anytime (withholding tax applies only on amounts above the annual minimum).
  • • Full Investment Control: Keep holding ETFs, stocks, bonds, or GICs while preserving remaining value for your estate.
Ideal for: Ongoing market growth, withdrawal flexibility & estate value
Option 02 · Guaranteed Income Zero Market Risk

Purchase a Payout Annuity

Lifetime or Fixed-Term Guaranteed Paycheque

Use part or all of your RRSP to purchase a contract from a life insurer that pays a guaranteed, predictable income stream for life (or a chosen term), insulated from market downturns.

  • • Eliminates Longevity Risk: Payments continue for as long as you—and optionally a surviving spouse—live.
  • • Pension Tax Credit Eligible: Qualifies for the $2,000 federal pension income tax credit starting at age 65.
  • • Trade-Off: Capital is permanently exchanged for income, with limited liquidity or estate residual unless a guarantee period is selected.
Ideal for: Covering essential fixed monthly expenses for life
Option 03 · High Tax Impact Use With Caution

Lump-Sum Cash Withdrawal

Full Deregistration into Cash

Deregister your RRSP and withdraw the balance in cash. The entire withdrawn amount is taxed as ordinary income in that single calendar year.

  • • Immediate Withholding Tax: 10% to 30% withheld at source, plus additional tax owing on your T1 return.
  • • Top Marginal Tax Spike: In Ontario, large lump sums can be taxed above 53% and wipe out Old Age Security for the year.
  • • When Appropriate: Generally suited only for very small RRSP balances or unusually low-income years.
Ideal for: Small balances or targeted low-income bridge years

Key Retirement Planning Strategies for RRSPs & RRIFs

01 · Income Splitting

Pension Income Splitting

Starting at age 65, allocate up to 50% of eligible RRIF or annuity income to a lower-income spouse on your tax return—and claim the $2,000 federal Pension Income Tax Credit.

02 · Spousal Age Election

Use a Younger Spouse’s Age

Before your first RRIF payment, elect to base mandatory minimum percentages on your younger spouse’s age to reduce taxable withdrawals and keep assets compounding longer.

03 · OAS Clawback Control

Strategic Early RRSP Meltdown

Gradually drawing down RRSPs between ages 60 and 71—while deferring CPP/OAS and supplementing cash flow with tax-free TFSA withdrawals—helps prevent OAS clawbacks after 71.

04 · Estate Continuity

Spousal Rollover vs. Terminal Tax

Naming a spouse or common-law partner as beneficiary allows a tax-deferred rollover upon death; otherwise, the full fair market value is taxed on the final return.
